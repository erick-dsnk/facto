# Ranking for the "combined" objective

**Summary**
- **#1 is Trusted Trade Agents (Valio Aimo), at 70.** It has the largest cash prize among the pure-software challenges (EUR 4,000 / 2,000 / 1,000, CHALLENGES.md:L681-693). It needs no hardware, and the mock API has been verified live. Its rubric (end-to-end demo 30%, controls 20%, exceptions 15%) rewards the same naive-vs-guarded measured before/after that the overall jury asks for. `valio-aimo` and `trusted-trade-agents` are **the same challenge** (both cite CHALLENGES.md:L547-693). They are listed twice below only because the research produced two files.
- **#2 is Make Blueprints Talk (67), #3 Earned Trust in AI Code (67) and #4 Decode the Noise (67).** All three have rubrics that score measured accuracy or evidence. That lets one build serve both the partner judges and the jury, and each stacks one strong tech challenge cheaply: Verda for Blueprints, Codex for Earned Trust, Tiger Data or Gemma for Decode.
- **Ranks 4-10 sit within about 2 points of each other (65-67) and should be read as a tie.** This band includes Personal Economic Trainer, Web Real-Time Database, Internet Butler, Rethinking Recruitment and AI Glasses. The competition estimates behind it are uncertain, and two competitive reports are missing (personal-economic-trainer and rethinking-recruitment). The idea the team picks will matter more than the order within this band.
- **AI Glasses has the best overall-win profile (jury fit 8/10) but the weakest partner prize:** a single "Best overall" award of Ray-Ban Meta glasses per member (CHALLENGES.md:L939-943). That is why it lands mid-table on the combined objective. If the team weights the overall win above the money, move it into the top three.
- **The robotics and hardware-kit challenges rank last** (Intel, Aaltonomous, Rokoko, Arduino) because of the physical-work penalty and partner rubrics that diverge from what the jury rewards.

## Criteria and weights

Each challenge gets a score of 0-10 on every criterion. The total is the weighted sum multiplied by 10, giving 0-100. All scores are **my estimates**. I used the research agents' 1-10 numbers as inputs, then re-checked them against the reports wherever they decided a placement.

| Code | Criterion | Weight | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | Partner-award prospects | 25% | Prize value (cash ranks above credits and gear), number of places, our skills fit, and expected competition. Competition is an estimate. |
| J | Overall-jury fit | 25% | The best direction in the research, scored against the jury rubric in 00-rules-and-jury.md §7: specific user, insight and depth, live demo, measured evidence, peer-floor legibility in 60-90 s, path forward. |
| A | Rubric alignment | 15% | How far one build serves both the partner rubric and the jury rubric without extra work. Leaderboard-gated or robot-run rubrics score low (00-rules-and-jury.md §7, "Combining with partner prizes"). |
| F | Feasibility and demo reliability for this team | 25% | Buildability in about 38.5 h, live-demo reliability, and external gating (licences, stations, shared devices). Includes the hardware penalty: none for software, a moderate penalty for a consumer device (glasses), a heavy penalty for robot or electronics kits, in proportion to the physical work. |
| S | Honest tech-challenge stacking | 10% | Natural, cheap stacks, weighted by prize: Gemini API EUR 4,000, Codex up to $10k credits, Verda $10k credits, Aiven EUR 1,000, Context.dev EUR 750 (tech-challenge digest). Only stacks where the sponsor's tool does real work count. |

Cross-cutting unknown: whether one project may enter **more than one main challenge** is unknown (00-rules-and-jury.md §4). No score assumes it can.

## The top six

### 1. Trusted Trade Agents / Valio Aimo (70.2)
This challenge leads because it scores well on all five criteria and badly on none. It has the largest cash prize among pure-software challenges (CHALLENGES.md:L681-693). It needs no hardware. The live mock API was confirmed to be fast and deterministic (trusted-trade-agents/feasibility.md). The partner rubric rewards an end-to-end flow, guardrails and exception handling (L659-679), so the measured "naive agent vs guarded interface over 50-100 requests" benchmark scores with both Valio and the jury's evidence lens. Its weakness is J (5.5): the opportunity report itself warns that B2B ordering "will read as niche protocol work" at the peer table. **What would change it:** a concrete kitchen-buyer story testing well at the table would raise J. Three findings would drop it below Blueprints and Earned Trust: many teams choosing it (expected interest is only "medium", an estimate); judging that will not accept a self-built order layer; or the cut-off and Wi-Fi demo risks proving hard to fix.

### 2. Make Blueprints Talk (67.2)
This one has the best rubric alignment of any software challenge. Accuracy, visual evidence and cost, each at 25% (L1607-1610), are measurable and map directly onto the jury's "distinctive insight" and "measured evidence" criteria. The feasibility spike gave exact IFC and DXF answers, and Verda is a natural, high-value stack. It ranks below #1 on prize size (EUR 1,000 plus a work trial, L1614-1624) and on the unknown evaluation protocol. **What would change it:** if Stoa confirms that the CubiCasa5K annotations may be used and explains how cost is measured, F and P rise and it could take #1. If evaluation turns out to be mostly raster counts on hidden data, with no detector allowed, it falls into the mid-table band.

### 3. Earned Trust in AI Code (66.8)
This fits the team better than any other challenge: we run coding agents every day. Tieto's Measurement (25%) and Evidence (20%) criteria are the jury's evidence lens. The sample branches give a ready-made, legible before/after: 222 of 237 files proven mechanical. Codex stacks honestly. It is held back by J (5): dev tools appeal less to the user-value jurors (Kia, Otto). Expected competitor interest is "high", which is an estimate. There is no feasibility report, so F 7.5 is my own estimate. **What would change it:** a feasibility check showing the C# repo builds and mutation tests run quickly would firm up F. Signs that many agent-tooling teams are choosing it would lower P.

### 4. Decode the Noise (66.5)
This is a low-risk, all-software build (feasibility 9/8). Its rubric puts 40% on detection quality (L76-79), so honest precision and FP-rate evaluation scores with the partner and with the jury. The "three-way traffic triage enforced on our own live server" idea is understandable in under a minute. It is capped by medium prize size and a theme whose obvious ideas (LLM propaganda classifiers) are common. **What would change it:** Hostinger confirming that traffic-bot detection counts as fully on-theme, and the size of the AI Router credits. If judges expect propaganda or social-text detection, the strongest direction weakens, and the alternative is limited by the academic-only PTC data licence.

### 5. Personal Economic Trainer (66.2)
The dataset is clean, and the build is mobile-first and in our skill set. The jury gets a specific user and a "verifiable numbers" insight. Verda explicitly names a banking coach (L2142), which gives a high-value stack with an open model run on EU infrastructure. The score rests on an unknown: the competitive report is missing, and "chat with your bank data" is an obvious idea, so P is a guess. **What would change it:** evidence that few teams pick it, or S-Bank mentors valuing the EU-hosted model angle, would lift it toward #2. Evidence that it is crowded would push it below #8.

### 6. The Web Is Your Real-Time Database (66.0)
This has the best stacking position: Best Use of Context.dev comes almost free with the main challenge. The sponsor's CTO sits on the grand jury, and a live web change triggering an action on stage is memorable. Its scores are pulled down by high saturation and an unresolved conflict over plan limits (Free plan 10 rpm vs "no rate limit", L2797). **What would change it:** confirmed raised limits and fast run-now monitors would raise F. Knowing whether jurors with sponsor ties recuse themselves (unknown) affects how much the Beer link is worth.

**Just outside the top six:** AI Glasses (65.2) is the pick if the team ranks the overall win above partner money. Its J and A scores are the highest in the field, and the Gemini stack is worth EUR 4,000. It would move into the top three if device access for the final demo were confirmed and the team discounted the non-cash Meta prize less.
