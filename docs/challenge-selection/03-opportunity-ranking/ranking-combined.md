# Ranking for the "combined" objective

**Summary**
- **Joint #1: Earned Trust in AI Code (Tieto, 72.5) and Trusted Trade Agents / Valio Aimo (72.3). Treat it as a tie.** Earned Trust is the best skills and jury fit: a measured before/after on the sponsor's own agent-written branches that developer peer reviewers will get immediately. Valio has the largest software cash pool (EUR 4,000 / 2,000 / 1,000, CHALLENGES.md:L681-693) and a rubric that pays for exactly the naive-vs-guarded evidence the jury wants. `valio-aimo` and `trusted-trade-agents` are **the same challenge** (both cite CHALLENGES.md:L547-693). They are scored once and listed together.
- **#3 Decode the Noise (69.8), #4 Make Blueprints Talk (68.5), #5 The Web Is Your Real-Time Database (67.8), #6 Rethinking Recruitment (67.0).** All are software-only, and their partner rubrics score measured quality, which lets one build serve the partner and the jury. Ranks 3-9 are within about 4.5 points. Those gaps are smaller than the uncertainty in the crowding estimates, so the idea we pick matters more than the order.
- **AI Glasses (62.8) has the best overall-win profile but the weakest partner prize** (one award of Ray-Ban Meta glasses per member, CHALLENGES.md:L939-943) and a device-access risk. It is the pick if the team weights the overall win above sponsor money.
- **The robot and hardware-kit challenges come last** (Rokoko, Arduino, Intel, Aaltonomous) because of the physical-work penalty and partner rubrics that diverge from the jury's. Spread is also near the bottom: its top-10 leaderboard gate and slides-only jury make it a poor overall-win vehicle.

Note: an earlier pipeline run left a draft ranking in `03-opportunity-ranking/_run1/`. It scored without the Earned Trust and AI Glasses feasibility reports and without competitive reports for Personal Economic Trainer and Rethinking Recruitment. Those reports now exist, so this ranking re-scores every challenge. The main changes are Earned Trust moving up (feasibility 9/8) and Personal Economic Trainer moving down (competitive report rates saturation "high").

## Criteria and weights

Each challenge is scored 0-10 on every criterion. Total = weighted sum x 10 (0-100). All scores are **my estimates**. The research agents' 1-10 numbers were inputs only, and I re-read the underlying reports where a number decided a placement (AI Glasses feasibility, Earned Trust feasibility/opportunity, the rules and jury digest).

| Code | Criterion | Weight | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | Partner-award prospects | 25% | Prize value (cash above credits above gear), number of places, our skills fit, how measurable the rubric is, and expected competition. Competition numbers are estimates. |
| J | Overall finalist and win potential | 25% | The strongest researched direction scored against the jury rubric in `00-rules-and-jury.md` §7: specific user 20%, insight and depth 20%, live demo 15%, measured evidence 15%, peer-floor legibility 15%, path forward 15%. Peer legibility is a gate: the jury only sees the peer-selected top 5. |
| A | One-build alignment | 15% | How far one build serves both the partner rubric and the jury rubric without extra work. Leaderboard-gated or robot-run rubrics score low (`00-rules-and-jury.md` §7, "Combining with partner prizes"). |
| F | Feasibility and demo reliability for this team | 25% | Buildable in about 38.5 h (Fri 19:30 to Sun 10:00, `00-rules-and-jury.md` §2), reliability of the live demo, and external gating (licences, stations, shared devices). Includes the hardware penalty in proportion to the physical work: none for software, none in principle for a consumer device (but device access counts as gating), heavy for robot or electronics kits. |
| S | Honest tech-challenge stacking | 10% | Natural, cheap stacks where the sponsor's tool does real work, weighted by prize: Gemini API EUR 4,000 first (CHALLENGES.md:L2530-2542), Codex up to $10k API credits (L2454-2466), Verda $10k credits (L2166-2168), Gemma EUR 1,000 to each of 3 teams (L2580-2584), Aiven EUR 1,000/750/500 (L2946-2958), Context.dev EUR 750/250 (L2889-2897). Gemini and Codex are open to every team, so they lift most software challenges by a similar amount. |

Cross-cutting unknowns: whether one project can enter **more than one main challenge** is unknown (`00-rules-and-jury.md` §4), so no score assumes it. The overall-winner prize amount is unknown (`00-rules-and-jury.md` §1), so J is valued as prestige plus the DeepMind "something special", not as a known sum.

## The top six

### 1=. Earned Trust in AI Code (72.5)
This is the challenge where our skills give the biggest edge: we run coding agents every day, and Tieto's question (when can a named human accept an agent-written PR without reading every line, CHALLENGES.md:L124-135) is our own problem. The partner rubric puts 50% on Measurement and Evidence (L241-255), which is exactly what Daksh, Beer and Arturs ask for, and the sponsor's branches provide a ready before/after: on `ju26-issue-2297`, 222 of 237 files collapse to three repeated lines plus one anomaly (earned-trust-in-ai-code/opportunity.md). The audience at the peer tables is developers, who feel this problem directly, so I scored J slightly above Valio. The feasibility report (9/8) found no hard blocker beyond getting the pinned .NET 8 SDK building in hour 1. It is held back by the modest prize (EUR 3,500 pool, L259-269) and by "high" expected interest from other agent-tooling teams (estimate). **What would change it:** many teams visibly choosing it, or Tieto signalling that the concept card matters far more than the measured experiment, would drop it below Valio. A slow or failing .NET build in hour 1 would lower F. Evidence that non-developer jurors (Kia, Otto) responded well to dev-tool finalists in past years would raise J.

### 1=. Trusted Trade Agents / Valio Aimo (72.3)
Valio scores well everywhere and badly nowhere. It has the biggest cash prize among software challenges (EUR 4,000 first, L681-693), no hardware, and a mock API the research verified live (trusted-trade-agents/feasibility.md, valio-aimo/feasibility.md). The partner rubric rewards an end-to-end flow, guardrails and exception handling (L659-679), so a measured naive-agent vs guarded-interface benchmark over real sponsor-data traps (allergen-changing substitutions, passed cut-offs, partial stock, sentinel stock values) scores with Valio and with the jury's evidence criterion at once. Its weak spot is J: both opportunity reports warn that B2B ordering reads as protocol work at a peer table unless a school or restaurant kitchen persona carries it. **What would change it:** confirmation that a self-built order and approval layer is acceptable, plus low visible team interest, would make it the clear #1 on prize value alone. A large returning field (Valio ran a Junction 2025 case, valio-aimo/competitive.md), or judging that depends on the live API during the Sunday 17:00 cut-off window, would lower it.

### 3. Decode the Noise (69.8)
A low-risk, software-only build (feasibility 9/8) whose rubric puts 40% on detection quality, including FP handling and explainability (L76-79). An honest precision/recall/FP evaluation against a baseline therefore earns partner points and jury points with the same work. The strongest direction, separating humans, legitimate AI agents and abusive bots on our own Hostinger server, enforced through the Hostinger firewall API and shown on a live "try to fool it" page, is legible at a table. It is capped by a mid-sized prize (EUR 3,500 pool, L83-97) and a theme whose obvious ideas (LLM propaganda classifiers) are common. **What would change it:** Hostinger confirming that traffic-bot detection is fully on-theme, and the AI Router credit size, would raise it. If judges expect propaganda or social-text detection, the strongest direction weakens, and the text alternative is limited by the academic-only PTC licence (decode-the-noise/feasibility.md).

### 4. Make Blueprints Talk (68.5)
The best rubric alignment of any challenge: accuracy, visual evidence, creativity and cost at 25% each (L1607-1610) are measurable and map onto the jury's insight and evidence criteria. The feasibility spike already produced exact deterministic answers on IFC and vector files at near-zero cost, with evidence overlays from the same computation. Verda is a natural, high-value stack (detector training), and Stoa is backed by juror Timo's fund (L1581, an indirect link only). It sits below the top three because the prize is small (EUR 1,000 first + work trial, EUR 2,000 pool, L1614-1624) and because 67% of public questions are raster counts, where a fine-tuned detector is the riskiest workstream. **What would change it:** Stoa confirming that CubiCasa5K annotations may be used and explaining how cost and accuracy are measured would raise F and P and could put it at #1. A hidden raster-only test set would push it into the mid-table.

### 5. The Web Is Your Real-Time Database (67.8)
The strongest stacking position: Best Use of Context.dev (EUR 750 first, L2889-2897) comes almost free with the main challenge, and Tiger Data and Gemini fit naturally. The sponsor's CTO, Beer, sits on the grand jury, and a live web change triggering an action on stage is memorable. It is pulled down by high saturation for watch-diff-alert ideas and the unresolved plan-limit conflict (Free plan 10 rpm vs "no rate limit", L2797). **What would change it:** confirmed raised limits and fast run-now monitors would raise F. Whether jurors with sponsor ties recuse themselves is unknown (`00-rules-and-jury.md` §9), and that decides how much the Beer link is worth.

### 6. Rethinking Recruitment (67.0)
Impact (45%) and UX (35%) dominate the partner rubric (L1870-1880), the prize is real cash (EUR 3,000 pool) plus a presentation to Espoo and the national talent-attraction network, and the problem (AI-written CVs screened by AI) is instantly recognisable at a peer table. The feasibility report found no technical blockers, and Verda names a recruitment tool as an example (L2142), which gives an EU-hosted open-model stack. It ranks sixth because the jury's depth criterion is the weakest fit, the low technical bar should attract many teams (crowding "medium", estimate), and any design that looks like ranking people breaches L1806. **What would change it:** Espoo confirming that mutual opt-in, explainable suggestions are acceptable under L1806, plus a measured counterfactual fairness test that holds up, would raise J and P. Signs of a crowded field would drop it below Personal Economic Trainer.

**Just outside the top six:** AI Glasses (62.8) has the highest J and A in the field and the most natural Gemini stack. It is the pick if the team ranks the overall win above partner money. It would move into the top three if the Meta booth confirms we can keep registered, capability-enabled glasses through Sunday judging, which removes most of the F penalty.
