# Senior-engineer evaluation: will the MVP work live? (Meta AI Glasses)

**Summary**
- Lens: the probability that each candidate's MVP **works reliably in a live demo on real glasses**, repeated many times through peer review, with only short slots on a shared device. Novelty, value and pitch are not scored here.
- Safest builds: **`glance-log` (82)**, **`which-way-now` (80)**, **`coverage-inspector` (76)** and **`did-i` (74)**. All four use generally available (GA) capabilities only, keep the cloud out of any real-time loop, use props we control, and work end to end on Mock Device Kit. `step-check` (66) is the safest of the fiducial-diff builds, which are more ambitious.
- Every candidate whose **core** depends on beta Motion or beta camera-stream audio scores 45 or lower. Motion is "available in the development and beta release channels only", orientation "can be `null`", and the docs do not say whether Motion and the camera stream can run together ([motion guide](https://wearables.developer.meta.com/llms.txt?product=all&version=1.0&document=products/dat/sections/guides/motion), opened). **`heads-up-sounds` is a hard reject**: its core input is beta, it needs a Display unit, and the noisy hall works directly against it.
- Every candidate shares the same platform risks: getting a device, the one-account pairing, the one-registered-app limit in Developer Mode, and `CAPABILITY_DENIED` (feasibility.md §4, §9). Those shift all scores together. The scores below differ only by risk specific to each candidate.

---

## 1. Criteria and method

Each score (0-100) is my **estimate** of P(the MVP as written in `_candidates.md` runs correctly at the table, on real glasses, through repeated peer-review runs). Inputs, in rough order of weight:

1. **Capability status.** A core built only on GA capabilities scores well above one built on beta. Beta capabilities may return `CAPABILITY_DENIED`, can only be used on dev or beta channels, and "do not resume after pause" (feasibility.md §3-§5). A core that depends on behaviour the docs leave unstated, such as Motion running together with the camera, is penalised as unknown.
2. **Perception difficulty at stream resolution.** The stream offers 720x1280, 504x896 or 360x640 at 2/7/15/24/30 fps over Bluetooth Classic. When bandwidth drops, the stream "first lowers the resolution by one step", then the frame rate, "but never below 15 fps" ([streaming](https://wearables.developer.meta.com/docs/develop/dat/streaming/), opened). The SDK reports no camera intrinsics (`_candidates.md` platform table). Large, high-contrast props that we design ourselves score well. Small text, fanned cards, utensil tips and fingertips score poorly.
3. **Latency placement.** A cloud round trip is about 2-5 s (feasibility.md §5, estimate). That is fine for deliberate queries. Any loop that must respond in under a second has to run on the phone.
4. **Short device slots and mock coverage.** Can 70-80% of the build, the evaluation set and the demo backup run through Mock Device Kit (feasibility.md §3)? Does the demo still work if we hold the glasses for only short slots?
5. **Venue conditions.** Bluetooth congestion, hall noise for anything audio-in, Wi-Fi for the cloud, and thermal and battery limits under repeated runs. Higher Motion rates "cost more battery" (motion guide). Real drain figures are unknown (feasibility.md §5).
6. **Integration count.** The number of separately fragile parts that must all work at once: HFP plus camera ordering, Display, a second phone, WebRTC, smart plugs, fine-tuned models.
7. **Display dependence.** The Display is on only 10 of the 20 units (CHALLENGES.md:L914-915). Combining Display with photo capture is a known bug, and each send redraws the whole view (feasibility.md §5).

`hard_reject` is set only when the core cannot be demonstrated reliably even with its stated fallback.

## 3. Evidence per candidate

**`glance-log` (82).** It uses GA camera streaming only. The dwell signal comes from on-phone optical flow, which needs no Motion. Asset identity comes from a large printed QR code, and the readings come from on-device OCR of printed digital meters, which are props we design for contrast and size. Nothing in the real-time loop touches the cloud. A cloud call is used only for the optional analog path, whose accuracy is unknown (`_candidates.md`), so the demo should use digital readings only. It works fully on Mock Device Kit with a recorded h265 walk-past (feasibility.md §3). A short device slot is enough to record clips and to rehearse once. The parts that can fail are false dwell triggers while the wearer's head pans, and QR decoding at 360x640 if the stream degrades. Both can be checked in hour 1 and tuned with props. Repeated runs at a table cost only a few seconds of streaming each. **Strongest objection:** it is reliable because it is easy. The core (QR plus OCR plus dwell) is close to a phone app with a head mount, so on stage a working demo may look trivial.

**`which-way-now` (80).** The core is GA camera streaming, a sharpness and text-density gate on the phone, then one VLM call. A 2-5 s answer (feasibility.md §5, estimate) is fine for "which platform?". We print the board ourselves, so the font size can be chosen to stay readable at the 720x1280 tier, and the 720p tier can be requested for the gated frames (streaming doc, opened). Live transit data is unknown and sits behind a mock, which removes an external dependency rather than adding one. It works on mock. The live risk is the gate firing on a blurred frame, and the stream degrading to 360x640 in a congested hall, which would make a dense 12-row board hard to read. Mitigations are fewer rows, larger type and the "move closer" error state. **Strongest objection:** the board is the hardest image in this group. If the hall forces 360x640, 12 dense Finnish rows at arm's length may not OCR reliably, and printing it in poster-sized type makes the demo less like a real board.

**`coverage-inspector` (76).** It uses GA camera streaming. Sticker zones (fiducial or coloured markers) make zone recognition a solved detection problem on the phone. "Covered" is a threshold on sharpness, apparent size and dwell time, all computed locally. Remarks go through the HFP mic and phone ASR, which brings in the HFP-before-camera ordering rule ("the audio route [can] fail silently", feasibility.md §3), but remarks are secondary and can fall back to a tap. Generating the PDF is plain web work. It works on mock. The remaining risks are tuning the thresholds so a fast pass does not count as covered, and sticker detection when the toolbox is flipped and tilted. **Strongest objection:** in the reliable sticker version the item has to be prepared with markers first, so a critic can say we are tracking stickers, not inspecting things. The sticker-free version, which would answer that, is where reliability falls apart.

**`did-i` (74).** The core uses GA capabilities only. Motion and Inputs are optional (`_candidates.md`). The verifier is a cloud call with a strict schema, run against safe and unsafe reference frames we capture ourselves, on a large stove-knob prop and deadbolt. A 2-5 s answer suits "Did I turn off the stove?". The weak link is the automatic attention gate (hand-on-object detection plus reference similarity at 360-504p). If it misses the moment, the answer is "not sure", which is honest but deflates the demo. A tap fallback is available, but the double-tap `Select` input is beta and is lost when the glasses are re-donned (`_candidates.md` platform table), so the realistic fallback is a phone button. It works fully on mock, and the 100-burst evaluation can be built from phone-camera footage. **Strongest objection:** the dangerous failure (a false "off") and the embarrassing one (a missed gate) both depend on a perception step at low resolution with motion blur. The design responds with "not sure", which keeps the system safe, but on stage it reads as a failure.

**`step-check` (66).** It uses GA only. An ArUco mat gives registration, and the alerts come from local before/after diffs, so latency is under a second without the cloud (an estimate in `_candidates.md`). The VLM sits off the critical path. Coloured tokens in an egg box are a controllable prop. But it chains several stages, all of which must hold at once: marker registration under head motion, hand-in/hand-out detection, region diffing, the step matcher, and an ambiguity check. The stabilisation offset means the image "does not exactly track the head rotation" (`_candidates.md` platform table), which affects diff alignment. ArUco detection distance at 504p is untested. It works on mock. Reviewer-injected errors are adversarial input, which is good evidence when it works and a public miss when it does not. **Strongest objection:** an open invitation for reviewers to plant errors will find the gaps in a 40-hour diff engine within the first few dozen table runs, for example a token half in a cell or a hand still in frame.

**`basket` (60).** GA only, with the VLM matching by category on 2-3 frames per event. The pick-up gate ("a new object enters the hand region and moves toward the basket") is a custom motion-and-hand heuristic, and it decides whether the silent-by-default system does anything at all. A missed pick looks like nothing happening. Reading allergen text at stream resolution is uncertain (`_candidates.md`). In the demo it can be avoided by having the VLM recognise a known product, but that weakens the honesty of the claim. The exit summary and list matching are easy. It works on mock. **Strongest objection:** the headline moment ("Contains hazelnut") depends either on reading tiny print, which is unreliable at 504p, or on the model recognising the packaging, which is not the claimed capability and fails on unfamiliar Finnish products.

**`panel-reader` (58).** GA only, with no uploads during use, so the venue network matters only at registration. That is a real reliability advantage. The live loop chains a homography to the reference panel, fingertip detection with on-phone hand landmarks, and projection onto the map at 7-15 fps. From a head-mounted view, the hand and finger cover exactly the part of the panel being read (`_candidates.md`), which hurts both the homography and the label. It needs a well-textured printed panel. It works on mock with the phone camera on a head strap, but whether the frames from that strap match the glasses' angle is an estimate. Registration should use the phone camera to avoid the Display-plus-capture bug. **Strongest objection:** a blindfolded peer moves fast and unpredictably. If the fingertip dot jitters between neighbouring buttons on the mirror screen, every observer sees the failure live.

**`sana` (57).** Recognising objects in hand is a VLM call. That is easy for 20 common categories, and a 2-5 s delay is fine. The fragile part is the voice loop: the HFP mic plus phone ASR grading Finnish speech in the hall, the HFP-before-camera ordering rule, and the reported iPhone 17 / iOS 26 HFP no-audio issue (feasibility.md §5). Turning HFP on also moves output off A2DP (feasibility.md §3). The SRS scheduler and the gate are simple. It works on mock except for real HFP behaviour. **Strongest objection:** the demo's payoff is "Oikein!" after a spoken Finnish answer. A first-time Finnish speaker's pronunciation in a loud hall is exactly the input that ASR grading gets wrong, so false "wrong" verdicts are likely during peer review.

**`teardown-memory` (55).** It uses the same GA fiducial-diff engine as `step-check`, plus a second registered surface (the tray), mapping parts from the device to tray cells, playing the log in reverse, and a verify diff for each step. That is more stages, and the targets are smaller (parts at least 1 cm; ArUco distance to be tested in hour 1, according to `_candidates.md`). Head motion while handling parts makes before/after alignment harder than on a static mat. It works on mock, and the evaluation can be recorded. The LLM labels parts asynchronously, so it adds no latency. **Strongest objection:** a teardown demo needs the wearer to stay at a consistent working distance for minutes. One mis-registered removal corrupts the ordered graph, and every later reassembly step inherits the error.

**`private-hand` (45).** It is GA, on the phone, with no network, which is good. But the core is a card detector fine-tuned during the event on frames of fanned, occluded cards at the glasses' angle (`_candidates.md`: 1-2 h of collection, estimate). The training data needs glasses-angle footage, so device time early. Reading partly hidden UNO corner indices at 360-504p under venue lighting is the hardest perception task among the GA candidates. The rules engine and TTS are trivial, and temporal voting helps. **Strongest objection:** a reviewer playing against the wearer will hold cards however they like. One wrong whispered "playable" card is visible to everyone at the table, and the detector was trained on a few hours of our own hands.

**`cross-contact` (44).** GA only, local CV. The core event is a utensil tip entering a zone, observed during fast scooping motion at stream frame rates. A fiducial on the spoon is small, blurred and partly covered by the hand. A missed contact is the safety-critical failure, and the demo invites exactly that test. The battery figure (about 1 h of streaming) is secondary reporting and an estimate (`_candidates.md`). Battery does not affect a 30-second demo, but repeated table runs plus thermal limits matter (feasibility.md §5). It works on mock. **Strongest objection:** a single missed peanut-to-safe contact during peer review discredits the whole safety story, and tracking tips at 7-15 fps through motion blur is the least reliable perception primitive in the set.

**`still-head` (44).** The engineering is the simplest of the Motion ideas: integrate gyro rotation over a putt of about 2 s at 60 Hz. The camera frame is optional, so the undocumented question of running Motion alongside the camera can be avoided. If Motion is enabled, I would expect it to work very reliably (estimate, around 75). But Motion is beta and limited to dev/beta channels (motion guide, opened), and it may be gated server-side (feasibility.md §4). There is no GA fallback for the core metric, since optical flow from a head camera cannot cleanly separate head rotation from ball motion (estimate). Mock replay of an IMU CSV proves the pipeline but not the try-it-on moment. **Strongest objection:** everything rests on one beta capability whose availability on the loaner glasses is unknown, and if it is denied there is nothing to demo.

**`show-me-once` (40).** The core is GA, but the demo is two-phase and live: author, segment with an LLM over keyframes and transcript in about 10 s, then guide a learner with done-state matching. It combines the HFP-before-camera ordering rule (silent failure), multimodal LLM latency in the middle of the demo, and done-state matching on deformable objects (a napkin fold), which is the weakest area for frame-similarity methods. The fallback, a pre-recorded expert clip, removes the live authoring that is the point of the demo. **Strongest objection:** both halves must succeed in sequence in front of a reviewer, and the second half's matcher is judging cloth folds at 504p, so the overall success rate is the product of two uncertain stages.

**`nod` (34).** The core needs Motion (beta) **at the same time** as the camera stream (for the dwell-selected object), and the docs do not cover that combination (motion guide, opened: "doesn't say whether Motion and camera streaming can run at the same time"). The nod/shake classifier must reject normal head motion across different peers after a 10-second calibration, and false accepts in a chatty peer-review setting are likely. The GA fallback (optical-flow nod) is described as "weaker, estimate". Mock replay loses the try-it-on moment. **Strongest objection:** two unknowns (Motion availability and concurrency with the camera) sit under a classifier whose failure mode, triggering on an ordinary nod in conversation, will show up in every peer review.

**`beacon` (30).** The core uses the beta Motion orientation quaternion, which "can be `null`" on some devices, at the same time as the camera (undocumented). It turns pixels into bearings through a self-calibrated focal length, because the SDK reports no intrinsics, and a stabilised image does "not exactly track the head rotation" (`_candidates.md`). Each of these adds bearing error, which the audio cue then guides the user along. Without orientation, integrating gyro drifts (estimate). The GA fallback (visual-only tracking) loses the property that makes the idea new, an IMU-rate cue. Variant (b) adds WebRTC (3-4 h, estimate). Mock IMU replay is deterministic, so a live demo needs real glasses for long, iterative tuning, which is the access pattern least compatible with short shared slots. **Strongest objection:** this idea has the most unknowns stacked inside the real-time loop, and it is impossible to tune without long uninterrupted time on a device we may not have.

**`table-talk` (24).** It needs a Display unit (10 of 20), beta Motion for focus, two or more participant phones joining via a web page, per-phone streaming ASR merged on a server, Display cards that redraw fully on each send with possible "lag due to Bluetooth bandwidth" (feasibility.md §5), and visual active-speaker detection. Each part is buildable. Five of them working together over hall Wi-Fi, with strangers' phones joining at every table visit, is the least reliable demo setup in the set. Display can be simulated (`_candidates.md`), but the mirrored demo loses the core benefit of captions in the line of sight. **Strongest objection:** every peer-review visit starts with strangers scanning a QR code and granting mic permission in a browser. That join step alone will fail or stall often enough to break the 60-90-second table window (00-rules-and-jury.md §3).

**`heads-up-sounds` (12, hard reject).** The core input is **beta** camera-stream audio, which is ambient and not beamformed (feasibility.md §3; `_candidates.md`). The output for Deaf users needs a Display unit, and whether Mock Device Kit simulates stream audio is unknown, so it is weak without glasses (`_candidates.md`). The demo plays a doorbell clip in a hackathon hall full of other sounds, which is the classifier's worst case, and the false-alarm counter will show it. There is no GA fallback for the core sensing. **Strongest objection (the reason for the reject):** the demo cannot be made reliable. The sensing input may be gated, it cannot be tested properly on mock, and the venue's noise works directly against it.

## 4. Engineering notes that apply to whichever candidate is picked

- **Hour 0-2 at the booth** (feasibility.md §10): settle device allocation, pairing and the account, the Developer Mode registration conflict, `CAPABILITY_DENIED` for Finland, and whether Motion is enabled and runs together with the camera. The last answer decides whether the conditional note in §2 applies.
- **Design for degradation.** The stream drops resolution first, then frame rate (but "never below 15 fps", streaming doc). Build every perception step to tolerate 360x640, and rehearse with a forced `LOW` setting.
- **No cloud in sub-second loops**, no Display unless the idea depends on it, and no combining Display with photo capture (feasibility.md §5).
- **Repeated demos:** stop the stream between runs (thermal states CRITICAL or above can deny sessions, feasibility.md §3), keep a charged spare, use a phone hotspot for the LLM, and have a Mock Device Kit h265 replay ready as a labelled backup.
