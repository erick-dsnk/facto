# Selected-citation review limitations

The schema array is ../broader-baseline-citation-review.json. Eight selected references map to six unique URLs and three unique definitive factual propositions; the mixed control repeats the Panama proposition and its same two references. Repeated references are retained as separate rows for matching, not independent evidence.

Seven references are verified for the exact checked relation; one is unsupported for the exact adoption event. No selected URL was inaccessible during this audit. Unsupported means the authentic UN registry records the different named event “conclusion”; it does not mean the registry text is false or its date/place metadata is wrong. This conservative reference-level scope judgment does not adjudicate the engine verdict or substitute the independently selected UNESCO text.

All six exact selected URLs were freshly requested and returned HTTP 200 substantive pages. This establishes current access through the execution environment's network path, not an uncached connection to each website's backend origin. Panama Canal Authority returned X-Cache HIT; UNESCO returned Age 3426. Other CDN/proxy caching may exist even without an explicit cache marker. Request times, selected response headers, redirect chains, byte counts and SHA-256 hashes are preserved in fetch-manifest.json and per-URL metadata files. The .body files are returned HTTP entity bytes, not TLS/wire captures. No bodies exceeded the 4 MiB capture cap.

Selected excerpts are provider-generated highlight serializations, not necessarily one contiguous byte-exact quote. Markdown headings/tables, ellipses, nonbreaking spaces and superscript footnote formatting differ from HTML serialization. The tested substantive passages were checked in fresh bodies. The UNESCO adoption heading and relevant clauses match substantively; the UN metadata cells match their labels. An isolated semicolon in a highlight conveys no factual evidence. The packet excerpt hashes were recomputed and all eight match exactly.

Institutional provenance is specific: Smithsonian museum history and institutional history are retrospectives; Panama Canal Authority describes its own opening; UNESCO supplies its legal text; UNTC supplies a treaty registration; the Federal Register agency profile is informational and the site disclaims being the official legal edition. No original 1914 ship log, 1846 statutory scan, or unselected linked instrument was inspected. Promotional environmental figures, historical population counts, and other incidental claims outside the checked proposition were not independently audited. The canal's business opening must not be described as proof that no earlier test passage existed.

Different selected publishers do not prove independent underlying origins. UNESCO and UNTC concern the same Constitution; Smithsonian and the Federal Register concern the same act. The reviewer previously authored the open development truth set, so this audit is not blinded or independent of dataset authorship. It is an independent selected-source check from the engine's construction and judgment: no implementation was inspected, no project key/provider calls were made, and no private cases were accessed.
