Smithsonian Institution Established

April 29, 1846

                            Image courtesy of the Library of Congress
                            James Smithson bequeathed his estate to the United States government with instructions to establish an institution in the nation’s capital “for the increase and diffusion of knowledge among men.”

                    On this date, the House passed legislation to establish the Smithsonian Institution, a research and cultural organization dedicated to science and the arts. Funding for the Smithsonian had come from a wealthy Englishman named James Smithson who had bequeathed his estate to the federal government with instructions to establish an institution in the nation’s capital “for the increase and diffusion of knowledge among men.” When word of Smithson’s gift reached the United States in late 1835, the House established a select committee, chaired by Representative John Quincy Adams of Massachusetts, to consider the terms of Smithson’s will. In 1836, Adams and the committee recommended that Congress pass legislation to allow President Andrew Jackson to appoint an agent to collect the bequest in London. But legal complications in England meant it took more than two years for the money to arrive in the United States. Over much of the next decade, lawmakers and everyday citizens debated what to do with the gift. Adams fought to create an astronomical observatory with the Smithsonian fund. Others petitioned to create a national university or an institute of agriculture. Some in Congress questioned the constitutionality of the gift altogether. In early 1846, the new chairman of the select committee on Smithson’s gift, Representative Robert Dale Owen of Indiana, submitted a bill to establish the Smithsonian Institution. During debate, Owen said the House had no choice but to use the proceeds of Smithson’s estate to charter the new institution. “We have no more right to put off . . . the appropriation of such a fund, than we have to direct it to our own private purposes,” he argued. On April 29, 1846, the House took up business on the Smithsonian once more, making minor adjustments to the bill text as different Representatives reviewed it. The final version passed the House 85 to 76. President James K. Polk signed the bill into law on August 10, 1846.
