# Independent scope annotation rubric

Determine whether the retained passage addresses the same referent, factual relation and material circumstances as the exact proposition. This is scope compatibility, separate from agreement, credibility, substantive content basis, origin independence and the final verdict. An incorrect proposition can have perfectly matching evidence scope.

An asserted value is what the proposition asks us to test: a date, number, location or named outcome can differ from the evidence while scope still matches. A date can instead restrict the period of an observation; an edition, age group, pressure, jurisdiction or standard can restrict the referent/population/conditions. Evidence for another such period/edition/population/condition does not automatically transfer. Identify which role the qualifier plays rather than treating every differing word as a mismatch.

A retained member counterexample can address and refute a universal claim over that population. A limited observation cannot by itself substantiate an unrestricted universal claim. A counterexample outside the claimed population does not address it.

For country/continent statements, use explicit retained geographic linkage rather than inventing a mapping from a country name. Different geographic granularity can still address a claim if the passage actually supplies the necessary relationship. A different entity, merely shared geographic vocabulary, unrelated dimensions or repeated numerals is not enough.

Use only the retained passage plus untrusted identifying title/URL context. Metadata can identify a document or subject; it cannot supply omitted dates, numerical values or factual provenance. Do not use adjacent source text that was not retained, remembered world truth or a publisher's prestige to fill a gap.

Return expectedScope:boolean, supporting substring, rationale and ambiguityFlag. If scope depends on disputed referent identity, implicit interpretation or unretained information, flag it for adjudication rather than forcing either label. This is open development; no provider outputs or candidate prompt/implementation have been used to author first labels.
