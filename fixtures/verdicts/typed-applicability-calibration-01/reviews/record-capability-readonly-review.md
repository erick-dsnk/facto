# Read-only review of the unwired record-capability mechanism

Reviewed `mechanism.md`, `prototype-api-spec.md`, the applicability-adapter
addendum, and the current manifest/snapshot/bind/applicability/evaluate code.
No prototype file was edited, no provider call was made, and no corpus input or
label body was inspected for this review. The root's reported 22 passing
applicability cases are not independently rerun or treated as production coverage.

The proposed separation is sound as an experimental boundary: a reviewed
document/version receipt can establish bounded provenance/competence, while
stance, substantive content, exact scope, confidence and final policy remain
separate. Receipts do not contain expected answers or claim regexes. Opaque
runtime handles backed by private WeakMaps prevent source-provided `verified`
objects, digests or serialized tokens from becoming trusted capabilities.

The local prototype validates installed content-addressed manifests/artifacts,
canonical URL/version and exact record-unit material alignment. Its token binds
claim, frame, excerpt, evidence ID and retained-context hashes; revocation and
manifest identity are checked. It records the original proof time, same origin
and no new accessibility/independence credit. Clipped qualifiers, normalized
highlights, unrelated same-host pages and forged proof strings must remain
unavailable. Admin-only manifest bootstrap must stay outside all user/source
inputs during any later integration; exported bootstrap functions alone are not
an application authorization boundary.

Three practical gaps remain unresolved:

1. **Raw English applicability.** `loadFrozenClaimFrame` resolves an installed
   exact-text/hash frame. This makes finite shadow evaluation executable, but
   does not route arbitrary admitted English. Installing those corpus frames as
   production exceptions would not be a generic solution. The proposed typed
   applicability item still needs separate calibration for event verbs, versions,
   periods, population, implicit currentness, negation and ordinal omission.
2. **Current-version proof.** The current implementation rejects `current` and
   unresolved time meaning. A dated canonical snapshot is not proof that a
   mutable current rule has not changed. Exa's bounded/cached/truncated excerpts
   do not provide a fresh canonical-origin body digest. No bounded fresh reader
   or authenticated current-version adapter exists here; a future one must fit
   existing attempts, bytes and the shared deadline.
3. **Existing content gates.** The receipt does not raise a .40 or .79 content
   assessment to .85. It cannot repair the UNESCO substance miss by itself.
   It can only address the independently reviewed ordinary-source route when
   every other gate already passes. BIPM's same-origin sufficiency issue, its
   final .79 quality assessment and implicit-current wording are distinct gaps.

Any integration must construct risk/conflict/origin context from trusted full
runtime evidence rather than caller-supplied booleans, then reapply all actual
policy guards, including risk confidence and lexical escalation. A capability
must not bypass an opposing witness, turn a copied archive into another origin,
or convert a signature/conclusion date into adoption or a ceremony into a first
ordinal. Every retained companion and bridge must remain available to policy.

Adding an applicability item inside the existing judgment call does not increase
call count, but does increase tokens, bytes, question load and binding complexity:
six added items can take a 55-question request to 61. The current full-context
experiments already approach the 64-KiB request ceiling. Resource/binding and
all non-target spillovers need measurement before any integration decision.

The prototype remains unwired and assigns no verdict. Passing finite
hand-framed proof controls can support another engineering review, but cannot
establish raw-English safety, fresh-current applicability, factual coverage or
backend readiness.
