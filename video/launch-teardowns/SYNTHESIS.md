# What 13 AI launch videos teach us

Sources: 12 AI-product launch videos found through xpoz (X, 2026, sorted by reach) plus our two style references, and the 47-minute YC interview where Farza explains his launch-video method. Each video was downloaded and analysed with a Deepgram transcript, every cut point, 1 frame per second, the first 6 seconds at 4 fps, a per-second loudness curve and silence detection. Agents only saw frames, so sound notes are read from loudness levels; nobody listened to the audio. Full per-video teardowns are in `group-a.md` to `group-d.md` and `farza-playbook.md`.

## Ten patterns that show up again and again

1. **Something moves in the first frame and a person or claim lands by second 3.** Bonzo walks into frame, Inkbox hands a prop to the lens, Odin's robot sweeps across the logo, Amplify's TikTok cards fly in on the first word. Farza picks his first frame on purpose because "most people see the first three seconds."
2. **Four hook shapes work:**
   - confession + number (Farza: "went super viral for an idea I thought was kinda terrible", 2.8M-views card at 5 s)
   - an enemy + a prop (Persona: "all building within the same two by six inch rectangle", phone raised)
   - a big number + a prop (Inkbox: "$5.3M SEED" over the founders)
   - a list of three scenarios (ElevenLabs: "to make an appointment / to book a job / to schedule a meeting… but nobody's around")
3. **The product is named between 0:09 and 0:15** in every human-led video except Bonzo (0:44, which is a story film). Farza's formula: *"This is who I am. This is what's going on. This is what I'm working on. Let me show you."* Setup is 10–20% of the runtime.
4. **The audio dips right before the reveal.** Farza drops to -19.6 LUFS at 12 s before "It's called Clicky". ElevenLabs dips before "until now". Inkbox leaves a 2 s gap before "Dear user". Persona dips before its privacy line. Granola and Bee bring the music in hard around 7 s, on the product name.
5. **Montages are fast and demos are slow.** Problem and proof montages run 1–1.5 s per shot (Bonzo 1.45 s average, Bee and Odin bursts of 1 s cuts on a spoken list). The demo holds: Farza 10.5 s, Bonzo 8.6 s, Odin two 13 s holds. Farza's best-performing demo had no cuts at all.
6. **Proof comes in 1-second hits across many contexts.** Farza shows the cursor in Resolve, Blender, chess and Figma. Odin shows a grid of hundreds of clips. Petrichor shows screenshots with a yellow highlight. Inkbox says "processed millions of messages".
7. **Every human-led video burns captions into every line.** Farza uses big white text mid-frame, Bonzo small karaoke subtitles, Petrichor serif captions, Inkbox small white subtitles. Big numbers and names get giant type of their own (Inkbox's stacked investor names, Odin's mint-green keyword per beat).
8. **One accent colour and one recurring motif.** Inkbox purple, Odin mint, Farza's blue cursor triangle, Bee's yellow. Odin starts the problem section in black and white and turns on colour when the founders appear.
9. **AI shows up as cutaways, never as the main footage.** Inkbox's bellhop appears in 5 locations, Petrichor uses painterly cells, Dock builds an office world. Each keeps one consistent character or world and returns to real faces in between. That matches our Omni plan.
10. **The ending is a person, a URL and one action, often with a joke.** Farza says "Please… I promise I'm not gonna look at your screen", Bonzo says "comment buddy", Inkbox ends with the team on the sofa, then the URL, then the logo. The logo card goes near-silent.

## What Farza says matters most (YC interview)

- **Audio over picture.** "If your audio is bad, I'm bouncing." 720p with crisp sound beats 4K with bad sound.
- **Script everything, then rehearse it out loud about 10 times.** Test: would you enjoy reading it if a friend texted it to you?
- **Hook test:** paste the first line into a mock feed and ask "did my eyes stop?"
- **Name the problem as your own, say "check this out", then cut to the screen.**
- **His stack:** Screen Studio for demos, DaVinci Resolve, a Sony FX3 with a DJI mic, and a filmmaker who does the motion graphics. He brings the script, the song and the vibe. He names no AI video tools.

## Our 2:00 beat sheet

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Hook | Moving first frame: a teammate walks into frame or hands the product (laptop/phone) to the lens | Fade up from near silence | Hook line as a big caption |

| 0:15–0:35 | Problem as our own | List-of-three montage, 1–1.5 s shots, **B&W**, plus 1–2 Omni cutaways with one consistent motif | Bed builds | One accent keyword per beat |

| 1:05–1:30 | How it works | Remotion line-art architecture + one on-camera tech line | Quieter bed | Labelled diagram |

## Shot list for Saturday

- Hook take ×5 (move into frame or hand the product to the lens). Pick the best.
- Founder intro at the window ×3, each line scripted and rehearsed.
- Problem montage: 8–10 one-second shots of the pain in real life (screens, hands, faces, the hackathon floor), to be graded B&W.
- Laptop screen filmed with the ZV-1 at an angle with shallow focus, plus a clean Screen Studio recording of the same demo.
- Teammate reaction shots (looking at the screen, small laugh, nod).
- Team two-shot on a couch or at the desk for the close.
- One blooper (leave the camera rolling after the last line).
- Room tone, 10 s.

## Omni cutaways, revised

Pick one visual motif tied to the product and keep it consistent across 2–3 shots, the way Inkbox's bellhop works. Each shot is 1–2 s and generated from text or a people-free still. Real faces stay real.
