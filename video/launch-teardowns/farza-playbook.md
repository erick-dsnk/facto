# Farza playbook (YC interview with Aaron Epstein). Timestamps are transcript seconds.
Source note: transcript has no mention of captions tools, AI video tools, or stock music sources. Frames not extracted. [INFER] marks my inference.

## 1. Formula for a video that converts
- Core formula [1303-1358s]: "My formula is always the same. This is who I am. This is what's going on. This is what I'm working on. Let me show you."
- Step 1, face first [752-762s]: "who is the person making this thing? Just show yourself. Show your face very clearly... confidently tell the camera what you're working on."
- Step 2, hook with a story hook plus a "why care now" [1358s]: "super clear hook. A very clear why you should care right now while you're on your scroll."
- Step 3, setup is 10-20% of runtime [670-700s]: "for a video that's one minute long, you need a setup, but it should only be like ten, twenty percent."
- Step 4, demo told as beats: problem from your own life, then the payoff (see 4).
- Audience of one [762s]: "at least one other person will think this is cool. And that's who I'm talking to."
- Build for yourself [2199-2212s]: "always building for the version of myself that's kinda watching out there. If I think that's cool, I think someone else would too."
- Demos, not launches [214-240s]: "I rather call these demos instead of launches... shipping just demos constantly." Work backwards from the launch: ask the hook and the Instagram caption before building [240-303s]. "we kinda know it's a hit before we even start."
- Raw "check this out" [940-955s]: "That check this out is exactly how you should be presenting yourself on the Internet." Polished launch only about once a quarter [956-963s].
- Convert, not views [421-471s]: 3M views gave 7,000 downloads, about 100 retained 5 days. "I do not care about the traffic at all."
- Posting mindset [1007-1025s]: "if this gets zero attention... I'm still happy I posted it." He posts and walks away, checks a week later.

## 2. Hooks
- Test [530-558s]: paste your text into a feed mock-up, "did my eyes stop?... Am I even gonna read the first sentence? Because if I don't read the first sentence, there's no way I'm going into this."
- First 3 seconds [558-621s]: "Most people when scrolling see the first three seconds." First frame of the Clicky video was purposely Xcode (target curious developers, not video editors), zoomed into the tiny triangle and moving it so something visibly moves: "I go, what is that?"
- Casual headline [472-478s]: "I built this thing. It's an AI teacher. What does it do? Why is it important?"
- First 10 seconds [662-700s]: "what you're doing, why it's important, let's get into it."
- Examples:
  - "Last week, I went super viral for something I built that I thought was kind of ridiculous... And now he's in Y Combinator" [1295-1303s]: self-deprecating, plus a niche signal (YC).
  - "It has been nine weeks since I built and launched my idea. It's an AI that can draw directly on your screen" [2286s].
  - "Two weeks ago I was in my apartment trying to figure out a cool idea... Two weeks later I built this thing now used by thousands of people every day. And the crazy part is..." [2308s].
  - "No hands. Ready?" [2134-2260s]: purposeful hook, visibly talking to the computer.
  - Targeted TikTok: "Are you the founder of a design agency? Stop right there. Let me show you something really cool." [1813-1860s]
  - Reel he studied: caption "two months ago..." on dark background with light on face; curiosity gap [1378-1413s].
- Study habit: "Am I bouncing? When am I bouncing?" Watches others' content to study.
- Platform-specific: Twitter watched muted; "Guy in front of blackboard, AI, draw screen" must parse in one glance [2036-2062s].

## 3. Scripting
- Every video scripted [840-864s]: "this whole video is scripted. Every line that I'm saying, I wrote like three days before." Rehearses lines in his head "in the shower, going to work... by the time I'm in front of the camera, I've already visualized it 10 times."
- Writing test [622-650s]: "If I like reading it, then it'll probably sound good if I say it out loud... would I read this if someone texted this to me?" "I'm the audience."
- Script only grounds the setup: [703s] "The worst part is when you're in front of the camera and you're like, what am I saying? You have nothing to ground yourself." His script shown: "Hi. I wanna share a new demo of our new agent interface..." then straight into the demo.
- Process [2706-2749s]: write about 10 minutes before recording ("the more you think through it before you get to the video, the better... you can catch a bad idea before it gets here"). Uses own app Freewrite (transcript spells it "Freerite", freerite.io, free, open source).
- Delivery: casual, direct to camera, problem stated as his own ("I'm not saying everyone has this problem... only talking about myself" [826s]). Self-deprecating, not "full of yourself" [1151-1179s].
- Length: no word count given. Typical video "one minute long" [670s]; produced Clicky video 4-6 hours with team; he can make a produced one in about an hour [1231-1294s]. [INFER] 1 minute at about 150 wpm is roughly 150 words, hook about 25-30 words.
- Write the line, not the story arc, for polish; "keep the story moving... not trying to keep your attention, that's the wrong way to look at it" [2217-2270s].

## 4. Demo into story
- "Everyone wants a story, and so how can a demo just be a mini story?" [1439-1454s]. The founder is a character the audience roots for [1455-1485s].
- Structure of his best demo (Spotify/Stripe, [2134-2270s], no cuts): hook ("No hands") -> small win (play AC/DC) -> escalation (turn it down 50%, responds while talking) -> payoff with a curiosity beat (open Stripe: "$140 in a day"). Aaron's reading: "there are beats."
- Screen-aware dictation [800-839s]: show the table-stakes version ("every other product... nothing special"), then "But here's the thing" and the problem you personally have, then "check this out".
- Show real use cases, first ones he had himself (Spotify, volume).
- Beats [2270-2310s]: "Am I here? Am I still watching?" Always ask where it's going.
- "Nothing special" is allowed: skip hand-wavy intros, life-of-a-founder content ("Never" [1544-1563s]). No fancy tour of the ending; show process.

## 9. Apply to a 2-minute hackathon video (rules; [INFER] marks derived)
1. Open on a face and a one-line "who I am, what I built, why you care" inside 10 seconds; no logo sting, no title card. First frame must have visible motion or a puzzling detail.
2. Write the whole script the night before; read it as if texting a friend; rehearse aloud so the talking head sounds off the cuff. [INFER] aim ~250-280 words for 2:00.
3. Make the hook a curiosity or self-deprecating line with a specific stat or time span ("48 hours ago...").
4. Spend only 10-20% (about 15-25 s) on setup; the rest is demo told as beats: small win, escalation, payoff.
5. State the problem as the speaker's own, then "check this out" and cut to screen. Skip the table-stakes version of the feature except one line.
6. Best moment of the demo in one continuous uncut take with real audio; a single cut in the demo is fine if beats keep moving. Record with Screen Studio.
7. Spend gear budget on audio: one clip-on or DJI-style mic; record voice separately if possible. Bad audio makes judges bounce.
8. Shoot talking heads with the Sony in one room with a soft key light and dark background (his reel reference) [INFER]; shoot 2-3 full takes of the hook and pick one; do not shoot b-roll beyond the process (team at laptops).
9. Show process: 5-10 seconds of the team building or reacting, with energy, not a polished montage. Keep AI cutaways to 2-3 and only for curiosity, since he uses none.
10. Write an actual beat map and watch the cut asking "Am I here? Am I still watching?" at each beat; cut anything where the answer is no. Export vertical and 16:9 [INFER], with burned-in captions for muted playback [INFER].
11. Ship even if it feels bad: "this is the worst video we've ever done" [864s] did 3M views. Give a teammate veto-proof posting rights.
