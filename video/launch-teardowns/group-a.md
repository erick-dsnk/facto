## 00a  "Episode 1" (wordisbonz), 1:30, 16:9, 62 cuts

**Verdict: human-shot, mixed with archival stock and screen captures. No AI-generated shots detected.** Real people, real locations, handheld lens flare and rack focus. The B&W trombone and audience footage looks like public-domain archive stock (my guess). The Instagram/YouTube screens are recordings or captures.

**Hook (0-3 s).** First words (0.6 s): "Hi. My name is Bonzo, and this is my girlfriend." On screen, a locked-off brick alley frames empty. Bonzo whips into frame from the right, blurred, stops, and turns to camera by 1.5 s with his name already captioned. You get motion, a face and a self-intro in the first second, shot warm and shallow like a film still. It sets "this is a short film" before any product appears.

**Beats.**
- 0-9 s: Character intro. Bonzo in the alley, a POV shot of him pulling a camera strap, a hand-held pocket cam flipping to show the girlfriend (producer and DJ), then a stylized pixel-glitch transition to her portrait.
- 9-18 s: Story and desire. "reminds me of another artist, Fred again... travel the world, and make music makes me wanna be creative like them." Montage: YouTube DJ clip, screen in a dark room, a green-lit studio jam, a car-hood selfie at a gas station, and a sunlit wood-floor DJ rig.
- 19-42 s: Problem. "learning a new software, especially music, is overwhelming." A Google search "how do i make music" pushes in close. A mouth close-up says "How the...". Dense Ableton UI is cropped. B&W trombones flash on "overwhelming". A FaceTime from the girlfriend ("Just watch a YouTube video. You'll be fine."). A tracking shot of YouTube scrubbing, with timestamps "10:15 Most replayed" and "11:39 Sidechaining". B&W audience on "takes me out of the experience". "I can't even ask questions."
- 39.7-50 s: Turn. "but there is something that might help." Alley door, elevator shots, Bonzo in an elevator, "Meet my friend Farza." (a real photo of Farza in a hat), laptop hero shot.
- 44.9-70 s: Demo. "Hey, Clicky. Can you teach me how to make a beat?" Ableton screen filmed off a tilted laptop, then a clean recording. Cyan "Thinking" bubble, "Click drums in the left sidebar, right where I'm circling it." Split frame with the Bonzo reaction face on the right side. About 15 s of AI voice, then "Okay. That's pretty much it. You finished your first beat." A YouTube cutaway of Fred again.. with the Clicky reply. At 78.3 s there is a single 0.3 s silence.
- 74-90 s: Payoff and CTA. Girlfriend laughing, concert glimpses in a vertical crop, car selfie ("it's just cool to finally start"), rooftop, café, the heyclicky site on a laptop. "comment buddy, and I'll send you the link to Farza's app." The ending is Bonzo in a doorway, camera in hand.

**Pacing.** 62 cuts in 90 s is about 0.7 cuts/s, average shot length about 1.45 s. Fastest at 24-35 s (about 14 cuts, the problem montage) and 79-90 s. Slowest holds are 50.98-59.56 s (about 8.6 s, the demo screen) and 54.6-59.6 s (5 s). The demo is the only place the film lets a shot breathe.

**Shot inventory.**
- Talking head: handheld medium close-up, shallow depth, shot outdoors in golden light. He moves, waves, walks in.
- Other speakers: girlfriend portrait, FaceTime screen, car selfies.
- B-roll ideas, each distinct:
  - POV hand and camera.
  - Product-on-hand reveal (flip-out screen).
  - Pixel-sort glitch transition.
  - YouTube browsing.
  - Dark-room monitor with a face glowing blue.
  - Studio jam, wooden floor.
  - Google search macro.
  - Mouth macro.
  - Trombone B&W stock (the "overwhelm" metaphor).
  - Audience B&W stock.
  - Phone scrubbing.
  - Elevator.
  - TransAmerica pyramid.
  - Rooftop.
  - Café.
  - Doorway ending.
- Screens: mostly filmed off a laptop at an angle and cropped, then cleaner recordings in the demo. Cyan UI overlays (circle highlight, Thinking bubble). The right half of the frame holds the reaction face during the demo.
- No device frames. No AI or VFX shots beyond the glitch transition and a color-pushed pixel wipe.

**Look.** Cinematic and warm: orange and teal. Amber elevator, green fluorescent studio, deep blue night club. Anamorphic-feel flares and soft blooms. Tilted laptop shots with grain. Locations are lived-in San Francisco. Each scene has its own gel color, so the film changes mood every few seconds.

**Text/graphics.** Word-synced white karaoke subtitles, lower-center, small, with no box. Almost every word is captioned. One logo-adjacent appearance (heyclicky site) near the end. Minimal motion graphics: highlight circle, Thinking bubble, timestamp labels.

**Sound (inferred).** Loudness holds around -10 to -16 LUFS with peaks to -7.8 (at 44 s, the "Hey, Clicky" call). That flat band suggests a music bed under the voiceover for most of the video; I cannot confirm that. Only one silence at 78.3 s (0.3 s), before the final reflective act. A dip to -20.4 at 18 s and -19.9 at 73 s shows two beat resets. The demo section (50-70 s) sits at -10 to -14, so the AI voice and music are hotter than the narration. The video does not use dead silence for drama.

**Vibe.** Warm, indie, funny, personal, confident. Leaves you feeling that starting something is easy and nice.

**What does the work.** (1) A named human with a relatable want (he wants to be creative like his girlfriend). The product is the answer to a feeling, not a feature list. (2) A new visual idea every 1.5 s keeps a 90 s explainer feeling like a film.

**Steal.**
1. Open on a person moving into frame with a name caption in the first second.
2. Cut a "problem montage" at under 1.5 s per shot: screen, mouth macro, search box, reaction.
3. Flip a gel color for each scene (warm, cyan, green). Cheap grading on the ZV-1 clips will do it.
4. Film your laptop at an angle with a split-frame reaction face next to the screen demo.
5. Use one stock or B&W shot as a metaphor for the problem ("overwhelming").
6. Close on a personal line ("it's just cool to finally start") and a plain CTA.

## 00b  Farza's Clicky reel, 0:55, 9:16 vertical, 31 cuts

**Verdict: human-shot, mixed with screen recordings and stock.** Farza is real, shot with a cinema-feel camera. The desert dunes and a "REAL-TIME" blurred text card read as stock or screen-grab montage. No AI-generated shots detected.

**Hook (0-3 s).** First words: "Last week, I went super viral for an idea that I built that I thought was actually kinda terrible." He sits at a round table in a sunlit window, hands on a laptop, with the "2.8M Views, 1.7K" tweet card flashing at second 5. Opens on a confession and a number. The contrarian line ("kinda terrible") stops the scroll. The hook sequence uses 0.1 s flash cuts around 1.1-1.3 s (artifacts of the tweet overlay).

**Beats.**
- 0-9 s: Hook and credential. "And this week I'm somehow in... Why comedy?" He holds a Y Combinator card to the lens (about 7-8 s), then "Y Combinator?!!" with an extreme close-up.
- 9-18 s: Reveal. "Let me show you what I'm building. It's called Clicky." Wide shot of him at a desk, a web text pane, a desert-dune background where a blue cursor triangle floats (the Clicky buddy as a cursor). "It's a little AI buddy that lives next to your computer cursor."
- 18-38 s: Demo. Color grading in DaVinci Resolve. Laptop filmed off-axis, then clean full-frame recordings with a blue "this one!" bubble. "Now you need to set the color space to Sony S-Gamut3.Cine." Five consecutive frames of a dropdown being scrolled.
- 29-45 s: Proof. "People are already using this to learn Blender from scratch, get coached while playing chess, get design feedback inside Figma, even write launch copy." Blender, chess board, Figma, Notion doc cycle at 1 s each, each with the blue cursor-buddy. Then real tweets scroll.
- 45-55 s: CTA. "Come and learn, and I'll send you a link... Please." Then he lies on a rug, camera shot from above, begging, then the joke tag: "I promise I'm not gonna look at your screen. It just captures it when you press the hotkey. Fuck." Ends on laughter.

**Pacing.** 31 cuts in 55 s, average shot length about 1.8 s, but the distribution is lumpy. A long hold (0-6 s, the first talking-head shot) opens the film, 18.65-29.13 s (10.5 s, the Resolve demo) is the slowest, and the proof run at 38-47 s is fastest (about 1 s per frame). The cut list undercounts the fast sections because the cuts are masked by overlays.

**Shot inventory.**
- Talking head: vertical medium shot, window light, Patagonia fleece and a cap, hands at laptop. A cleaner "behind the monitor" shot appears at 11 s.
- B-roll ideas: tweet card with a view count, YC card to lens, extreme close-up, wide of him at the desk, desert dunes with a floating blue cursor, headline macro text, Resolve laptop filmed at an angle, keyboard finger macro, timeline recording, cursor/dropdown scroll, Blender, chess, Figma, Notion, tweet stack, rug top-down gag.
- Screens: mix of filmed screens (warm, off-axis) and full-bleed recordings. The blue cursor-buddy with a "this one!" label is a consistent motif, so the UI itself becomes the character.
- No AI or VFX shots visible.

**Look.** Warm daylight, creamy and soft, a gently teal-pastel palette. Crisp and filmic: the same ungraded look as a lifestyle ad. The rug shot from above has a rotating-feel color grade.

**Text/graphics.** Big white bold subtitles in the middle of the frame, synced to words (more of a TikTok/Reels sizing than 00a), no boxes. Overlay cards for tweets. The blue triangle cursor works as the brand mark.

**Sound (inferred).** Loudness starts hot (-10 to -7.4 at 0-5 s, with a peak at 5 s on the tweet), drops to -19.6 at 12-13 s (a gap before the product name), and climbs into the CTA (-8.9 at 38 s). A second dip to -20 at 45 s and -23 at 48 s sets up the final "Please." joke, then peaks at -7.7 at 50 s. There are no long silences and no silence flags. I infer light music or none, with speech carrying the rhythm; a real musical bed is not confirmed.

**Vibe.** Self-deprecating, quick, funny, sunny, sincere. Leaves the feeling that this is a charming founder you want to help.

**What does the work.** (1) The "kinda terrible / 2.8M views" confession hook with a number on screen. (2) The product shown inside apps people already know (Resolve, Blender, Figma, chess) in about one second each, with the same cursor-buddy.

**Steal.**
1. Hook with a surprising number plus a confession.
2. Show the product in 4-6 contexts, 1 s each, with one recurring UI motif.
3. Keep the founder to camera in a sunny window, no extra gear.
4. End on a self-aware gag ("I promise I'm not looking at your screen").
5. Use a quick hold on the only screen demo you need (10 s), then fast cut.
6. Put captions mid-frame, large, word-by-word. Remotion does this easily.

## 02  Persona launch (Zach Yadegari), 1:24, 16:9, 2 detected cuts

**Verdict: human-shot talking head plus produced product visuals. No AI-generated footage detected.** The band shots on a white background look like studio footage or a 3D-looking product render (uncertain which), and the phone UI is a screen composite. Persona is a company with a hardware band, not a video-first story.

**Hook (0-3 s).** First words: "Every major AI company is racing to build the best assistant, but they're all building within the same two by six inch rectangle we wear in our pockets every day." On screen, a static medium shot of Zach at a desk in a polo, plant to the left, panel to the right, hands open. At 3 s he lifts a real phone into frame. The line is a big-claim stat, delivered with a prop. There is no b-roll in the hook.

**Beats.**
- 0-14 s: Problem and thesis. Static talking head. "We believe AI is supposed to free us from our screens, not make us use them more. So we built a new interface."
- 14-18 s: Reveal. A white flash dissolve, then the Persona logo, a lineup of band colors, "Persona Band." The scene goes bright white.
- 18-35 s: Demo 1 (gesture). "simply flick your wrist or hold down on the face, then ask anything." Wrist on white, a "Listening" pill, "Hey. Can you ask Mac how many preorders we got today?" Close-up of a green ring spinning, and "Sir, I just emailed Mac. I'll let you know his reply."
- 35-66 s: Feature list. Zach on the left with a phone-UI panel (an iMessage-style mock) on the right: Uber from the airport, cancel subscriptions, negotiate bills, DoorDash, "the only AI assistant that can make phone calls on your behalf."
- 63-68 s: Triple-click note-taking mode on the wrist, with a "Note Taking Mode Enabled" pill.
- 68-78 s: Privacy. Back to the talking head. "This isn't a continuous listening band. The microphone will only activate under your control."
- 79-84 s: CTA. White background, bands in four colors and "yourpersona.com". "Preorder your band and start texting your persona today."

**Pacing.** cuts.txt lists only 28.47 and 68.10 because the dissolves and picture-in-picture swaps fool the detector. Counting by eye on the sheets, I see about 15-20 visual changes in 84 s, an average of about 4-5 s per shot. The slowest is the 0-14 s talking head. The fastest is the 14-18 s reveal run (logo, bands, wrist in 4 s).

**Shot inventory.**
- Talking head: static, locked-off, eye-level, medium shot, desk in frame, hand gestures. The only camera move is Zach's body.
- B-roll: product logo card, band color lineup on white, wrist flick on white, close-up of a green ring, finger tapping the band, two-fingered triple-click, a note-mode pill.
- Screens: phone-UI mock in a white iPhone frame, overlaid on the left third beside the speaker (about 39-62 s). Conversations play in real time as he talks about them.
- No AI shots. Product clips on white are clean and slightly synthetic.

**Look.** Flat, soft, evenly lit. Muted sage and mint tones in the office, with a white infinite background for the product. Consumer-tech product page, not cinematic.

**Text/graphics.** Italic white lowercase captions at the bottom, small, on the talking-head shots and in a ghosted gray on white. The Persona logo is a simple mark with the wordmark. Clean UI pills ("Listening", "Thinking", "Ready to speak") built as overlays.

**Sound (inferred).** Loudness is steady around -15 LUFS in speech. Dips at 26-28 s (-25), 30-34 s (-21 to -25) and 67 s (-26.8) come from silence around the wrist demo and the pause before "We believe in absolute privacy". The wrist demo has the quietest levels in the video, consistent with a hushed AI voice and little music. The end falls to -42.5 and -54.8 (true silence at 83.55 s). I infer there is no strong music bed (levels stay flat and speech-only), but cannot confirm.

**Vibe.** Calm, polished, earnest, product-page, a little corporate. Leaves the sense of a real product from a young founder with a clear idea.

**What does the work.** (1) A single clear thesis in the first 14 s ("free us from our screens"). (2) Cutting to the product on a pure white background, so the object is the hero.

**Steal.**
1. Open with one sentence that names an enemy ("same two by six inch rectangle") and use a physical prop.
2. Run a locked-off ZV-1 talking head; the founder's hands do the editing.
3. Dissolve to white for the product reveal, then film the thing on a white sheet.
4. Use a UI pill overlay ("Listening / Thinking") as a Remotion component.
5. Put the phone or app UI as a card beside the speaker while he explains.
6. End on a clean card with the URL and a single call to action.
